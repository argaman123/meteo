The lead (2026-06-10): "make sure from time to time that what I think is good, is actually good, and let me know when I ask for something that isn't that much agreed upon conventionally. I'm experienced but I'm here to learn."

**Why:** he wants the project built on genuinely good choices, not just patterns he happens to know; he explicitly invites pushback.

**How to apply:** when a request deviates from widely-agreed convention, implement his call but say plainly "heads-up: most teams do X here, because Y" — once, briefly, at decision time. Periodically sanity-check earlier locked choices too. Don't be sycophantic about choices that are merely fine. See [[concise-ask-dont-choose]] and [[breathing-tree-rule]].

**Addendum (2026-06-10):** the same candor applies to MY recommendations — when presenting tech choices, state maturity and risk explicitly, never just strengths. He felt the maplibre+deck+weatherlayers stack was "disguised as perfect" after a globe-path vendor bug surfaced ("stable API" ≠ battle-hardened; the globe path is young). Trust took a dent; every future stack-level claim should carry its honest risk note (the posture is now recorded in docs/decisions.md "Stack maturity").
